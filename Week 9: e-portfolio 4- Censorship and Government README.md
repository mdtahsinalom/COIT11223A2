### Summary of the Artefact

The video examines the relationship between **government and censorship**, and explores how governments can regulate and/or limit access to information. It draws attention to the impact of censorship on freedom of expression, the free flow of information, and the rights of individuals to express their views.

Additionally, the artefact demonstrates how governments can use the justification of national security, public safety or harmful content to promote censorship. However, too much censorship can lead to a lack of rights and transparency.

In general, the video helped me to have a better understanding of the ethical issues involved in maintaining freedom of speech while still enforcing government regulations.

### Justification on Why I Chose the Artefact

I selected this artefact because it clearly demonstrates the connection between government and censorship and why it is important in society. I found it interesting because there are both positive and negative aspects of censorship.

Censorship is a practice that governments can use for national security, public safety, or preventing harmful information. However, it can also limit freedom of speech and access to information.

This artefact helped me consider the issue of balancing the protection of the population with individual rights and freedom of expression.

## Artefact 2: A News Article I Found This Week

<img width="940" height="508" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9c72f315-6bd3-44df-8938-9a43803a5ca2" />


### Article URL

[Wells open to changing digital harm bill after Coalition censorship concerns – ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-13/wells-willing-to-change-digital-harm-bill/107147806)

### Summary of the Artefact

The Australian Government is proposing new laws to place responsibility on online platforms to mitigate foreseeable harm online, as described in this ABC News article.

The proposal may also enable the Communications Minister to add more categories of **"serious online harm"** to the list and has sparked concerns from the Coalition about potential government censorship and limitations on freedom of expression.

However, Communications Minister Anika Wells said the government is willing to review aspects of the proposal, including possibly allowing parliament to determine what constitutes a serious harm.

In general, the article illustrates the difficulty of balancing online safety, government regulation, and freedom of expression.

### Justification on Why I Chose the Artefact

I selected this artefact because it is a recent Australian example where government regulation, online safety, and freedom of expression are being debated.

It was interesting because the proposed digital harm laws aim to protect people from harmful information online, but they have also raised concerns about censorship and government control of information.

This article made me realise that creating laws for online safety is not easy because it involves difficult ethical choices. It also made me think about the appropriate role of government in protecting people without unnecessarily restricting freedom of expression.

## Artefact 3: Scholarly Article

<img width="940" height="571" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0994dda5-098f-4943-82a0-8d214e4a5f00" />


### Article URL

[Selective Control: The Political Economy of Censorship](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2022.2074587)

### Summary of the Artefact

This academic article explores the mechanisms of censorship by governments and other parties as they seek to influence media and information.

The researchers studied approximately **9,000 incidents of censorship in 196 countries from 2001 to 2015**. The study revealed that there is a tendency towards selective rather than blanket censorship of media.

Journalists and media outlets that have a significant readership and/or impact may be more likely to experience serious censorship. Media connected with protests and other forms of collective action may also face greater risks.

In general, the article illustrates how censorship can influence access to information, public discussion, and political participation.

### Justification on Why I Chose the Artefact

I selected this artefact because it offers an academic perspective on the use of censorship in controlling information and media.

It was interesting because the article explains that censorship can be selective, and some journalists, media outlets, and politically important information may be subject to greater restrictions.

This relates closely to the Week 9 workshop, where we learned about government censorship and how censorship can be used to influence information or restrict dissent.

The article was useful in helping me understand that censorship is not only about removing harmful content but can also have important consequences for freedom of expression and access to information.

### Artefact #4 – Key Idea Learned in the Workshop

An important concept I learned during the workshop is that censorship involves balancing two important values: **public safety and freedom of expression**.

Governments can block or regulate online content to protect people from harmful or illegal content, but too much blocking or regulation can prevent people from accessing information and expressing their opinions.

I also learned that censorship has the potential to be used in ways that could lead to the control of information and suppression of dissent.

I learned that it is an ethical question to consider which content should be restricted, as governments, technology companies, and internet users may have different opinions about what should be available online.

### Justification on Why I Chose This Artefact

I chose this key idea as an artefact because it was one of the most important things I learned from the Week 9 workshop.

Prior to the workshop, I was mainly aware of censorship as governments censoring, blocking, or deleting information. However, I learned that censorship is more complex because it involves balancing safety, freedom, privacy, and access to information.

I was also interested in the question of who should have control over what is available on the internet — governments, technology companies, or internet users.

This artefact helped me realise that censorship is a difficult ethical issue and that restrictions designed to protect the community can also be viewed as limitations on individual freedom.
