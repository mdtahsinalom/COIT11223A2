### Summary of the Artefact

The Guardian article discusses a major copyright settlement involving Anthropic, authors and publishers regarding the use of copyrighted books in developing artificial intelligence systems.

The publisher of the Harry Potter series, Bloomsbury, has more than 14,000 titles included in the settlement and may receive millions of dollars in compensation.

The article highlights the growing dispute between AI companies and content creators regarding the use of copyrighted material to train AI systems. It also demonstrates the importance of intellectual property rights and raises ethical questions about whether AI companies should be permitted to use creators' work without permission or compensation.

I selected this artefact because it provides a real-world example of the relationship between artificial intelligence and copyright.

I found this article interesting because it connects intellectual property with modern technology and illustrates the possible consequences of unauthorised use of protected material. It also increased my awareness of the importance of respecting creators' rights when developing and using AI systems.

### Artefact #4 – Key Idea Learned in the Workshop

An important concept I learned from the workshop was the importance of **human authorship for copyright ownership**.

The Monkey Selfie case involved a monkey pressing the shutter button of a camera and taking a photograph of itself. One of the main questions raised by the case was whether an animal could own copyright.

I learned that animals cannot hold copyright under U.S. copyright law because copyright protection generally requires human authorship.

I found this discussion particularly interesting because it can also be connected to current debates surrounding AI-generated content, including the question of who owns the rights to AI-generated artwork, text and other creative works.

### Justification on Why I Chose This Artefact

I selected this workshop idea because the Monkey Selfie case helped me understand the concept of copyright ownership through an interesting real-world example.

It encouraged me to think about who should be considered the owner of creative work when that work is not directly created by a human.

I believe this issue is particularly important today because artificial intelligence is increasingly being used to generate text, images, music and other forms of digital content.
